Where Cold Outreach Actually Fails

The copy is rarely the problem. The list is. A dirty list produces bounces, bounces produce reputation damage, and reputation damage makes the next campaign harder than the last one.

📮

Bounces cost more than the campaign earns

Dead addresses and misspelled domains sit in exported lists permanently. Sending to them spends domain reputation on recipients who were never going to reply.

🧹

Cleaning is manual and inconsistent

Headers vary between exports, names arrive glued together, addresses show up written as bob (at) acme (dot) com, and the same lead appears twice under a different company spelling.

⚖️

Compliance gets remembered after sending

Unsubscribe language, sender identity and a physical address are required. Reviewing them by eye across hundreds of drafts is how a violation ships.

What It Does

Five stages, each doing one job, all runnable as commands you can put in a script or a cron job.

🧽

Normalize and dedupe

Header variants, Chinese and Western name splitting, anti-scrape spellings restored to real addresses, dedupe by email and by company plus person, empty rows dropped.

🌐

Live MX validation, no dnspython

A DNS resolver written in the standard library checks whether the domain actually accepts mail. Verdicts separate ok, role, typo with a suggested correction, risky and dead &mdash; because a DNS failure is not the same thing as a bad address.

🎯

ICP fit scoring

A 0-100 score with A/B/C/D bands from decision-maker titles, company domain, role accounts and record completeness. Used for ordering only, never for silently filtering leads out.

✍️

Personalized drafts, not mail merge

Templates render per lead with fallbacks for missing fields. Flags catch leftover placeholders, empty slots and AI tells before a draft reaches your outbox. Failing emails are not written at all.

⚖️

CAN-SPAM check per draft

Unsubscribe mechanism, sender identity and postal address are checked on every rendered message rather than reviewed by eye.

🔁

Track to the reply

A state machine with enforced transitions. Bounces and unsubscribes land in a suppression list automatically and filter every future list, so you do not have to remember.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does this send the emails for me?

No, by design. It produces validated, compliance-checked drafts and tracks state; sending stays in whatever client or API you already use. That is why your mail credentials never enter the tool.

Do I really not need to install anything?

requirements.txt is empty on purpose. The DNS resolver is a small standard-library implementation, so there is no dnspython, no dig binary and nothing to break on a different operating system.

Is the validation as good as a paid email verifier?

It does real MX lookups, which catches dead domains, typo'd domains and domains that refuse mail. It does not do SMTP mailbox probing, so it cannot confirm an individual inbox exists. MX-level validation is the honest ceiling without touching the recipient's server.

What does it do with bounces and unsubscribes?

They enter a suppression list automatically, and every future list is filtered through it. The state machine enforces valid transitions so a lead cannot silently move from bounced back to sendable.

Where does my data go?

Nowhere except DNS. Addresses stay in local CSV and state files on your machine; the only outbound traffic is the MX lookup needed to validate a domain.

Is there a free version?

Yes. The free Lite edition does the cleaning and the live MX validation, as a separate download. The paid edition adds ICP scoring, personalized draft rendering, the per-draft CAN-SPAM check and the CRM state machine.
